What usually breaks

Independent hotels usually have a property management system that produces a good report at three in the morning and very little you can act on at two in the afternoon. So the front desk keeps its own picture: a printed arrivals list, a paper housekeeping board, and a radio.

  • The numbers are a day old. Occupancy, ADR and RevPAR come from a nightly export, which is exactly when they stop being useful.
  • Room readiness lives on paper. Housekeeping marks a board that reception cannot see, so guests wait for rooms that are already clean.
  • Maintenance is reported verbally. A broken shower gets mentioned on the radio and remembered by nobody in particular.
  • Guest notes are scattered. The thing the returning guest told you last time is in a system nobody opens at check-in.

What we build

For a hotel the four modules are usually the front desk view, the room status grid, housekeeping, and maintenance, all reading from the same live state rather than from an export.

  • Front desk. Today's arrivals and departures with room readiness and guest notes attached, plus live occupancy, ADR and RevPAR against the same night last week.
  • Room status grid. Every room across every floor with its current state, replacing the board.
  • Housekeeping. Assignment and completion tracked in the same place reception can see it.
  • Maintenance tickets. Raised against the specific room they affect, with a status, so nothing depends on somebody remembering.

A property management system feed is nearly always one of the two integrations, and it's the one we check feasibility on first, because access varies enormously across PMS vendors.

What changes

Reception can tell a guest their room is ready because the screen already knows. The nightly numbers become live numbers, so a soft night is something the duty manager can respond to rather than read about tomorrow. Maintenance stops being an oral tradition.

Common questions

Can it connect to our PMS or channel manager?

Sometimes, and it depends entirely on your vendor. Some property management systems have a usable API, some have a nightly export and nothing else, and some are effectively closed. This is the first thing we establish on the kickoff call, because it's the difference between a straightforward build and one we'd rather scope carefully.

Does it take bookings or handle payments?

No. It's an operations dashboard, not a booking engine, and it doesn't process card payments. Your PMS and channel manager keep doing that. What this adds is the live operational picture on top, which is the part those systems are typically weakest at.

Will housekeeping staff use a dashboard?

Only if it's faster than the board, which in practice means very few taps on a phone to mark a room clean. If it's slower than a pen, the board wins and the tool fails. That constraint shapes the housekeeping module more than anything else.
